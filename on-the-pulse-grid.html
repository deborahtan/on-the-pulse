Measuring Marketing Impact

Marketing Science & Experimentation
Cited Aug 2026 — Academic research

Platform-reported lift can be off by a factor of 13, peer-reviewed research finds

A widely-cited comparison of observational (platform-reported) versus randomized-experiment estimates of ad effectiveness found that in half of 15 Facebook experiments studied, observational estimates were off by a factor of three or more — in one case, a true 2.4% lift was reported observationally as 1,306%. A separate documented case put reported ROAS above 4,100% while the randomized experiment on the same spend returned minus 63%.

Implication

This is ammunition for exactly the CFO conversation most marketing leaders are already losing: if platform-reported lift can overstate true impact by an order of magnitude, then any board-level ROI number sourced solely from platform dashboards is a defensibility risk, not just an optimism bias. The fix — independent experimentation, not better dashboards — is a budget line, not a reporting-cadence change.

Aug 11, 2026 — Industry tracker

Measuring visibility in AI search is now its own software category

At least ten distinct tools (Profound, AthenaHQ, Peec AI, Otterly.ai, Knowatoa, Scrunch AI, Brandlight, Goodie AI, and AI-visibility add-ons from Ahrefs and Semrush) are now competing to measure how often and how accurately brands are cited inside ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google's AI Overviews — each using a different methodology.

Implication

Before allocating budget to any AI-visibility vendor, know that the entire category has no agreed methodology yet — a spend decision here is a bet on one company's proprietary counting method, not on a benchmarked industry standard. Worth treating any vendor's visibility score the way a CFO would treat a projection with no audited methodology behind it.

Activation

Attention & Media Consumption
Early Aug 2026 — Industry analysis

AI agents are becoming a second audience brands write for

New industry analysis argues that content increasingly needs to serve two audiences at once: the human who eventually reads it, and the AI system — search engine, shopping agent, recommendation assistant — that decides whether to surface it at all. The recommendation is to write around explicit decision cues ("best for," "choose this when") that both people and AI systems can use to shortlist an option.

Implication

This changes what marketing is actually buying when it buys reach: a growing share of what used to be direct human exposure is now brokered by an AI system with its own selection logic, which means budget aimed purely at human attention may be increasingly mis-targeted regardless of channel. Worth a genuine strategy review, not a content-guidelines update.

Read source — Davler Media →
Early Aug 2026 — Industry-wide

Attention is being redefined from proof-of-click to proof-of-exposure

Advertisers are increasingly able to build audiences from users who were simply shown an ad — with no click, site visit or consented identifier required — turning previously invisible mid-funnel exposure into a usable signal, industry analysis of 2026's media technology shortlist finds.

Implication

This quietly changes what counts as a paid outcome, which is a finance question as much as a media one: if impressions without action are treated as equivalent to genuine engagement in vendor reporting, budget can get allocated against a weaker signal than the CFO believes it's paying for. Worth confirming with any vendor exactly what their 'attention' or 'exposure' metric is actually counting before renewing that line item.

Market Structure

Vendor Consolidation
Aug 6, 2026 — New York

Nielsen is buying DoubleVerify for $2.15 billion

Nielsen Holdings and DoubleVerify entered a definitive agreement for Nielsen to acquire DoubleVerify in an all-cash deal at $13.60/share, a 30% premium to DoubleVerify's 60-day average price. The combined company is expected to generate over $4 billion in pro forma revenue, expanding Nielsen's addressable market to $300B+. The deal is expected to close around Q1 2027, pending shareholder and regulatory approval.

Implication

This is a direct vendor-risk event for any organisation relying on DoubleVerify for independent verification: one of only two vendors with genuine scale in that category is being absorbed into a larger holding company, reducing the number of truly arms-length options in the market. Worth a procurement review of measurement-vendor concentration risk now, not at next contract renewal.

Regulatory

Data, AI, PII & Consent
Aug 2, 2026 — Brussels

EU makes AI-content labeling law, not policy

Article 50 of the EU AI Act took effect, requiring AI-generated synthetic content shown to EU users to carry a machine-readable marking, with deepfakes needing a distinguishable label.

Implication

This is now a board-level compliance line item, not a marketing guideline: it's the first hard legal mandate for AI-content labeling anywhere in the world, and it applies regardless of who generated the content — the agency, the brand's own team, or a platform's AI tool. CDOs should confirm now whether existing content pipelines can actually produce the required disclosure, not assume creative or legal already has it covered.

Aug 1, 2026 — Sacramento

California enabled mass data-broker deletion

The Delete Act's centralised deletion system went live — one consumer request now removes their data from every registered California data broker at once.

Implication

This is a data-supply risk to flag to whoever owns audience planning and budget: a single, centralised deletion mechanism shrinks the reliable pool of third-party and appended data industry-wide, which means any budget still allocated against broker-sourced audiences is funding a pool that's actively shrinking and getting less reliable in real time.

Aug 2026 — London

ICO enforcement action shows what "reasonable care" actually means

The ICO issued the Metropolitan Police Service with an enforcement notice and reprimand after an officer emailed 18 people affected by a sensitive case with all recipients visible in the "To" field, potentially exposing highly sensitive inferences about them.

Implication

This sets a precedent CDOs should route to legal immediately: liability turns on what can be inferred, not on whether personal data is technically named. Any organisation building or sharing lists from sensitive segments — health, legal, financial — carries the same exposure this enforcement action punished, regardless of sector.

Aug 6, 2026 — WARC (World Advertising Research Center)

Social-first marketing has a growth ceiling, new research finds

WARC's "Pace Principle 2.0" finds that social and creator marketing — for all its speed and cultural relevance — hits three structural ceilings that no amount of additional content or paid media can break through without balancing it against longer-term brand-building.

Implication

This is independent evidence a CMO can put directly in front of a board or CFO that's been pushing hard toward short-term, high-frequency social spend: the research comes from a source with no commercial stake in the channel-mix answer, which makes it more defensible in a budget conversation than anything from a platform with an obvious interest in the outcome.

NZ privacy and cyber law is shifting personal liability onto executives

New Zealand legal analysis flags a new landscape of privacy penalties and personal liability under NZ cyber law, as regulatory expectations tighten around who is accountable when data protection fails.

Implication

This is the sharpest possible version of a board question a CDO or CMO in New Zealand should be raising proactively: liability is shifting toward the individual decision-maker, not just the organisation. Worth confirming personally, not assuming, that governance and sign-off structures have been updated to reflect that — waiting for legal to raise it first is the wrong order of operations.
